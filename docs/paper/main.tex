% --- abstract ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
\noindent We adapt the Biogeochemistry-Informed Neural Network architecture \citep{xu2025binn} from terrestrial soil carbon to marine biogeochemistry, replacing ECCO-Darwin's Green's-functions calibration of six parameters \citep{carroll2020ecco,carroll2022attribution} with gradient-based per-cell parameter inference. A small per-cell neural network maps environmental covariates to the Carroll-N parameters, sigmoid-bounded into Carroll's published physical ranges, fed through a differentiable PyTorch reimplementation of Darwin's source-minus-sink (SMS) kernel, and trained end-to-end against ECCO-Darwin v05 observations. We report a v3.1 sweep across 857 seeds in 86 configurations of 3-area-of-interest joint training (Equatorial Pacific, North Atlantic Subpolar, Southern Ocean Pacific). Two findings: the iron pair (iron dust solubility \texttt{alpfe}, iron scavenging rate \texttt{scav\_rat}) recovers reproducibly at 38/40 (95\%) Cal-grade with the F2 configuration, the first reproducible iron-pair joint recovery in our framework; and a structural 5/6 ceiling holds across the sweep set --- 0/857 seeds reach all six Carroll-N parameters within Cal-grade, with only two single-seed 5/6 events unreproduced at $n{=}20$. A composition test of the two complementary 5/6 lever families fails to reach 5/6 (0/10), regressing below either parent. We interpret the ceiling as parameter conservation under the available observational constraints rather than a method failure, and discuss it as a predictive diagnostic for future observation-design choices. Code and full sweep outputs are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

ECCO-Darwin \citep{carroll2020ecco,carroll2022attribution} is the data-assimilative coupling of the MIT general circulation model \citep{mitgcm1997} with the Darwin marine ecosystem module, providing a global ocean biogeochemistry state estimate on the ECCO LLC270 grid (nominal $\sim$1/3$^\circ$ horizontal resolution, 50 vertical levels, 1992--2017+). The biogeochemistry side exposes 103 independent tunable scalar parameters in source code, of which six --- iron dust solubility \texttt{alpfe}, iron scavenging rate \texttt{scav\_rat}, small phytoplankton growth \texttt{Smallgrow}, large phytoplankton growth \texttt{Biggrow}, diatom palatability \texttt{diatomgraz}, and PIC/POC production ratio \texttt{R\_PICPOC} --- are calibrated via Green's functions \citep{menemenlis2005gf}.

Green's-functions calibration is structurally limited in two ways. First, each tuned parameter requires a full perturbation-and-rerun campaign, so the calibrated subset is small ($\sim$6 of 103 in Carroll's published configuration). Second, the calibration is implicitly tied to its observational input set: adding a new observation channel (for example, future PACE ocean-color retrievals or expanding GEOTRACES coverage) requires a fresh Jacobian campaign rather than a reuse of the existing one.

We address both limitations by making the biogeochemistry differentiable. A small neural network predicts per-cell parameter vectors from local environmental covariates; gradients flow from the observation loss through a differentiable PyTorch reimplementation of Darwin's source-minus-sink kernel back to the network weights. New observation channels enter as additional loss terms --- a configuration change rather than a perturbation campaign.

The methodological precedent is the Biogeochemistry-Informed Neural Network (BINN) of \citet{xu2025binn}, who introduced this pattern for terrestrial soil-carbon calibration of CLM5: a small MLP predicts 21 soil-carbon parameters per location, outputs are sigmoid-bounded into physical prior ranges, and a differentiable PyTorch reimplementation of CLM5's matrix-form steady-state equations enables end-to-end backpropagation from a soil organic carbon profile loss. We adapt the same pattern to marine biogeochemistry --- per-cell ocean fields, Carroll-N parameters, and ECCO-Darwin observation channels.

This paper reports a workshop-scale proof-of-concept: framework, experimental protocol, and the empirical landscape that emerges from a 857-seed sweep across 86 single-lever configurations of 3-area-of-interest joint training. The headline empirical finding is a structural 5-of-6 calibration ceiling supported by four independent pieces of evidence. The headline methodological finding is reproducible joint recovery of the iron pair (\texttt{alpfe}, \texttt{scav\_rat}) at 38/40 seeds in our best configuration. We argue that the 5/6 ceiling is informative --- it predicts which future observation channels would be unblocking --- rather than a method failure.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item A BINN-style architecture adapted to marine biogeochemistry, including a per-cell 1$\times$1-convolutional MLP (\texttt{DINN}, $\sim$454 weights) for the cleanest structural comparison against the global-scalar Green's-functions class, and a deeper variant (\texttt{DINNDeep}, $\sim$9.4\,k weights) for higher-quality within-region fits.
  \item A differentiable PyTorch reimplementation of Darwin's source-minus-sink kernel over five prognostic tracers (baseline) and seven (carbonate-enabled extension, with the Follows-2006 carbonate solver \citep{follows2006carbonate} and the Wanninkhof-2014 gas-transfer law \citep{wanninkhof2014gas}).
  \item Empirical characterization of the recovery problem at scale: 857 seeds across 86 configurations, with reproducibility analysis at $n{=}40$ on the best configuration.
  \item Identification of a structural 5/6 calibration ceiling, supported by four independent evidence pieces; identification of binary observation-anchor mutexes (any non-zero PIC absolute anchor wipes iron-pair recovery) that explain the ceiling.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{ECCO-Darwin and the Carroll-6 calibration target}

The Darwin module's source-minus-sink (SMS) kernel --- the per-cell rate equations that compute the time derivative of each prognostic tracer due to biology and chemistry, separately from physical transport --- is implemented in the Fortran subroutine \texttt{darwin\_plankton.F} in both the Carroll 2020 Darwin 1 build and the upstream \texttt{darwinproject/darwin3} repository \citep{mitgcmcontrib2020,darwinproject2024}. Physical transport (advection, diffusion, mixing) is handled by MITgcm and inherited as-is from the ECCO state estimate.

Carroll 2020 \citep{carroll2020ecco} tuned six parameters of the SMS kernel via Green's functions: \texttt{alpfe}, \texttt{scav\_rat}, \texttt{Smallgrow}, \texttt{Biggrow}, \texttt{diatomgraz}, and \texttt{R\_PICPOC}. Carroll 2022 \citep{carroll2022attribution} updated the model to Darwin 3 / v05 but \emph{inherits the same six calibrated values bit-for-bit} --- verified by reading the Darwin 1 \texttt{code\_darwin/} source and the Darwin 3 \texttt{input\_darwin/data.darwin} namelist files side by side. The six values therefore define our calibration target.

\subsection{BINN: differentiable parameter inference for biogeochemistry}

\citet{xu2025binn} introduced BINN for soil-carbon calibration of CLM5. The architecture is: a 4-layer MLP (3 hidden $\times$ 128 neurons) outputs 21 raw values per location, parameterized sigmoid bounding (Equation~4 of their paper) maps them into per-parameter prior ranges (Equation~5), the bounded parameters enter a matrix-form differentiable CLM5 (Equation~7, vectorized in PyTorch) integrated to steady state (Equation~8), and a smooth-L1 soil organic carbon profile loss with a cosh prior penalty (Equation~10) backpropagates to the MLP weights. Training is 300 epochs, AdamW with learning rate $0.01$, on $25{,}925$ SOC profiles from WoSIS. BINN's reported $\sim$50$\times$ speedup over PRODA's per-site Metropolis MCMC reflects the substitution of gradient-based for sampling-based parameter inference.

We adapt the BINN pattern from terrestrial to marine. Key differences:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item Marine biogeochemistry has per-cell environmental forcing of qualitatively different character (currents, mixed-layer dynamics, light attenuation, dust deposition, sea ice), motivating a per-cell parameter prediction rather than per-site.
  \item Six Carroll-N parameters rather than 21 CLM5 parameters, matched to the canonical ocean-BGC calibration target.
  \item Five to seven prognostic tracers in the differentiable forward, capturing the dominant iron and carbon cycling but reducing the 39-tracer Darwin SMS kernel \citep[per the param inventory in our companion documentation,][]{eccodarwininventory2026} to a tractable subset. Reducing this proxy gap is the natural next iteration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Concurrent ML approaches to ocean biogeochemistry}

Neural-BGC \citep{oualalachkar2026neuralbgc} emulates dissolved oxygen and nitrate from a six-dimensional physical state via cascaded neural networks, coupled to ROMS by replacing the prognostic transport-reaction equation entirely. The architecture is a pure-NN emulator with no parameters in the Darwin sense; the methodological overlap with DarwinDiff is therefore disjoint. Meunier, Ouala, and colleagues \citep{meunier2025veros} port the Veros ocean dynamics to differentiable JAX, occupying the differentiable-physics-only direction without addressing parameter recovery within biogeochemistry. The differentiable physics-informed neural network (PINN) family in general descends from \citet{raissi2019pinn}, but DarwinDiff and BINN both differ from canonical PINN in not using PDE residuals as soft-constraint loss terms --- they use the mechanistic model as the differentiable forward instead.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Differentiable box-model SMS kernel}
\label{sec:method-sms}

We reimplement Darwin's SMS kernel in PyTorch over five prognostic tracers (baseline): dissolved iron (\texttt{DFe}), small phytoplankton biomass ($P_s$), large phytoplankton biomass ($P_l$), particulate organic carbon (POC), and particulate inorganic carbon (PIC). The governing equations (Equations~\ref{eq:dfe}--\ref{eq:dpic}) are
\begin{align}
  \frac{d\,\text{DFe}}{dt} &= \alpha_\text{fe}\,\Phi_\text{dust} - r_\text{scav}\,\text{DFe}\cdot\text{POC} - Q_\text{Fe}(\mu_s P_s + \mu_l P_l)\,f_\text{Fe}(\text{DFe}), \label{eq:dfe} \\
  \frac{dP_s}{dt} &= \mu_s\,f_\text{Fe}\,P_s - m_\text{lin}\,P_s - m_\text{quad}\,P_s^2, \\
  \frac{dP_l}{dt} &= \mu_l\,f_\text{Fe}\,P_l - m_\text{lin}\,P_l - m_\text{quad}\,P_l^2 - g_\text{diatom}\,G_0\,P_l, \\
  \frac{d\,\text{POC}}{dt} &= M_\text{tot} - w_\text{sink}\,\text{POC}, \\
  \frac{d\,\text{PIC}}{dt} &= R_\text{PIC:POC}\,M_\text{tot} - w_\text{sink}\,\text{PIC}, \label{eq:dpic}
\end{align}
with the Michaelis-Menten iron limitation $f_\text{Fe}(\text{DFe}) = \text{DFe} / (\text{DFe} + K_\text{Fe})$ and $M_\text{tot}$ the total phytoplankton-loss flux. The six Carroll-N parameters \{$\alpha_\text{fe}$, $r_\text{scav}$, $\mu_s$, $\mu_l$, $g_\text{diatom}$, $R_\text{PIC:POC}$\} are learnable; the remaining ten variables (\texttt{M\_LIN}, \texttt{M\_QUAD}, \texttt{G0\_GRAZE}, \texttt{W\_SINK}, \texttt{K\_FE}, \texttt{PHI\_DUST}, \texttt{Q\_FE}, light, mixed-layer depth) are held fixed at literature-plausible defaults matching Carroll's choices.

For carbonate-enabled experiments we extend the system to seven prognostic tracers, adding dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) and total alkalinity (ALK):
\begin{align}
  \frac{d\,\text{DIC}}{dt} &= -\mu_s\,f_\text{Fe}\,P_s - \mu_l\,f_\text{Fe}\,P_l - R_\text{PIC:POC}\,M_\text{tot} - F_\text{CO\textsubscript{2}} / H_\text{mld}, \\
  \frac{d\,\text{ALK}}{dt} &= -2\,R_\text{PIC:POC}\,M_\text{tot}.
\end{align}
The air-sea CO\textsubscript{2} flux $F_\text{CO\textsubscript{2}}$ is computed at each step from $(\text{DIC}, \text{ALK}, T, S)$ via the Follows-2006 carbonate solver \citep{follows2006carbonate} and the Wanninkhof-2014 gas-transfer law \citep{wanninkhof2014gas}. $F_\text{CO\textsubscript{2}}$ is a diagnostic, not a prognostic tracer; carbon is conserved within the box up to the air-sea flux and particulate sinking export.

Time integration uses forward Euler with $\Delta t = 0.25$\,d for 200 steps to a near-steady state, matching the climatological framing of the comparison against time-mean Darwin output.

\subsection{Per-cell DINN architecture}
\label{sec:method-dinn}

The Darwin-Informed Neural Network (DINN) is a per-cell 1$\times$1-convolutional MLP. The 1$\times$1 kernel commits the architecture to per-cell parameter independence: every cell's predicted parameter vector depends only on that cell's own environmental input. Two production variants are used.

\paragraph{\texttt{DINN} baseline ($\sim$454 weights).} Input is per-cell normalized sea surface temperature (1 channel). Two hidden layers of 16 channels each with $\tanh$ activations, followed by a final 1$\times$1 convolution to 6 output channels. Used in our structural-ceiling experiments where the cleanest comparison against the global-scalar class matters more than absolute fit quality.

\paragraph{\texttt{DINNDeep} production ($\sim$9.4\,k weights).} Input is a 4-channel stack of SST, mixed-layer depth, surface windspeed, and latitude. An input projection to 32 channels feeds four residual pre-norm blocks (per-cell LayerNorm, GELU, 1$\times$1 conv, repeat) followed by a final projection to 6 channels. Per-cell semantics are preserved by a custom LayerNorm that normalizes only over the channel dimension at each spatial location.

\paragraph{Sigmoid bounding.} The raw NN outputs are mapped into Carroll's published parameter ranges by
\begin{equation}
  \theta_i = \ell_i + (h_i - \ell_i)\,\sigma(\hat{\theta}_i),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $[\ell_i, h_i]$ are Carroll's published physical bounds (for example, $\texttt{alpfe} \in [0.05, 1.0]$ and $\texttt{scav\_rat} \in [3\times 10^{-8}, 3\times 10^{-6}]$\,s$^{-1}$). The bounding guarantees biologically sensible parameter values regardless of NN output magnitude and conditions the optimization landscape.

\subsection{Training loop}

Per epoch: (i) DINN forward, $\text{env} \to \hat{\theta}_\text{raw}$; (ii) sigmoid bounding, $\hat{\theta}_\text{raw} \to \theta$; (iii) box-model integration from a uniform initial state, $\text{state}_0 \to \text{state}_T$ over 200 forward-Euler steps with per-cell parameters $\theta$; (iv) loss, z-scored mean-squared error between predicted state and Darwin v05 climatological target over ocean cells; (v) backpropagation through the 200 integration steps via autograd; (vi) Adam optimizer step on the DINN weights. We train for 1500 epochs at learning rate $5\times 10^{-3}$; one fit completes in $\sim$7--8\,min on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 Laptop.

The z-scoring decouples calibration from absolute scale: the network is required to match the spatial pattern of the target field, not its absolute magnitude. Absolute-units anchors (PIC and POC) are available as separate loss components and are studied as observational interventions in our experiments (§\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Multi-area-of-interest joint training}
\label{sec:method-multiaoi}

v3.1 extends the framework to joint training across three areas of interest (AOIs): Equatorial Pacific (HNLC), North Atlantic Subpolar, and Southern Ocean Pacific. Each AOI contributes its own loss; the optimizer must find Carroll-N parameter fields that satisfy all three simultaneously. Two architectural variants:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item Shared DINN with an AOI-identity input channel: a single network is conditioned on a per-AOI one-hot identifier and learns regional parameter regimes implicitly.
  \item \texttt{PER\_AOI\_DINN} with consistency penalty: separate networks per AOI, with a soft cross-AOI consistency loss term controlled by the hyperparameter $\lambda_\text{consistency}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Observation channels}

The v3.1 loss combines per-AOI z-scored MSE on chlorophyll across five phytoplankton functional types, particulate organic carbon, dissolved inorganic carbon, alkalinity, and air-sea CO\textsubscript{2} flux; plus absolute-units MSE against GEOTRACES IDP2025 surface and subsurface dissolved iron, GEOTRACES subsurface POC, and GEOTRACES surface biogenic silica (via the steady-state diagnostic in \texttt{src/darwindiff/silica.py}). Optional PIC and POC absolute-unit anchors are studied as observational interventions in §\ref{sec:results}. Loss weights are configuration-level hyperparameters varied systematically across the sweep.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:method-sweep}

We executed a five-wave chained sweep covering 86 single-lever configurations and 857 seeds total, followed by a Wave 6 composition test (1 configuration, $n{=}10$ seeds). Each configuration is one runner invocation with 10 batched seeds (\texttt{torch.compile} amortizes JIT across the batch). Per-configuration outputs are JSON files at \texttt{D:\textbackslash runs\textbackslash bcr\_*\textbackslash <config>/}. The sweep waves cover (Wave~1) Basin C refinement with PIC/POC dose-response, paired anchors, chlorophyll-weight response, Southern Ocean weight, architectural variants, carbonate, and longer training; (Wave~2) orthogonal-lever sensitivity across GEOTRACES weight, POC subsurface weight, PINN drift weight, FET weight, and $\lambda_\text{consistency}$ with $n{=}30$ robustness; (Wave~3) two-dimensional grids in POSI $\times$ AOI weight and CHL1 $\times$ POSI; (Wave~4) integration-step depth tests; (Wave~5) drill into the 5/6 breakthrough events with $n{=}20$ robustness; (Wave~6) composition test of the two complementary 5/6 lever families.

\subsection{Binary mutex in observation anchors}
\label{sec:results-mutex}

Any non-zero PIC absolute anchor (\texttt{PIC\_ABS\_W}, tested down to $0.02$ with paired POC anchors at multiple ratios) wipes iron-pair recovery to 0/10, regardless of magnitude. POC anchor alone also kills the iron pair, with a different downstream basin geometry. The mutex is \emph{binary in PIC presence, not graduated in magnitude}. We interpret this as the PIC absolute anchor jointly constraining the product $R_\text{PIC:POC} \cdot M_\text{tot}$ via a tight basin, pulling the optimizer to a region where \texttt{R\_PICPOC} is Cal-grade but the iron pair must drift to balance the loss.

This is the most actionable predictive finding for future observation-design choices: whether a candidate observation channel can coexist in the loss without inducing mutual mutex with iron recovery is a non-trivial question, and it is testable a priori by adding the channel to DarwinDiff's loss and running a small sweep.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The 5/6 ceiling as parameter conservation}

Across 857 seeds in 86 single-lever configurations, no seed reaches 6/6 Cal-grade. The framing that fits the evidence is parameter conservation: the available observational constraints support approximately five effective degrees of freedom over the 6-parameter Carroll-N surface, and the sixth parameter is always the residual sink --- loss weighting decides which. The structural ceiling is supported by four independent pieces of evidence (§\ref{sec:results-ceiling}), and the failure of the natural composition test (§\ref{sec:results-wave6}) is the strongest negative evidence: the two complementary 5/6 lever families do not compose, instead regressing below either parent.

This is informative for the broader question of which Carroll-N parameters are recoverable from ECCO-Darwin's v05 observational basis. The 5/6 ceiling does not say ``the method is broken''; it says ``the current observational constraints are insufficient by one dimension.'' Observation channels carrying information orthogonal to the current set --- for example, satellite particulate inorganic carbon for the carbonate counter-pump, or additional GEOTRACES traverses constraining the iron pump --- are the natural unblock. DarwinDiff's framework makes such tests cheap: each new candidate channel is a loader and a loss weight.

\subsection{Comparison with Green's-functions calibration}

Even with the structural ceiling, DarwinDiff exhibits substantial methodological advantages over Green's-functions calibration:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item One gradient pass per training step replaces $\sim$12 model runs per Gauss-Newton iteration. The 1500-epoch DINN training run on a single GPU completes in $\sim$7\,min, compared with multi-day full-model perturbation campaigns.
  \item New observation channels enter as additional loss terms rather than as a fresh Jacobian campaign. The marginal cost of adding a candidate channel is a loader and a loss weight.
  \item Per-cell parameter variation is strictly more expressive than the global-scalar class. The DINN class contains the global-scalar class as the constant-prediction limit, so DINN's loss is at most equal to the optimal global-scalar fit, and typically lower whenever the target field has spatial structure expressible only by per-cell parameters.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}

The current results are subject to several caveats.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Box-model proxy.} The 5--7-tracer box reduces the 39-tracer Darwin SMS kernel. The 5-PFT $\to$ 2-PFT collapse is the dominant recovery-bias source in our companion experiments (Section~\ref{sec:future}). Eliminating the proxy via a port of \texttt{darwin\_plankton.F} to differentiable PyTorch is the natural next step.
  \item \textbf{Per-cell architecture, no spatial coupling.} Real ocean BGC has advection and diffusion between cells, which the per-cell DINN ignores. This is appropriate for parameter recovery (the truth structure is per-cell) but not for a Track-2 emulator direction.
  \item \textbf{Climatology only.} All current fits use 23-year time-mean Darwin output. Time-resolved fitting opens a different scientific question (dynamical recovery, not steady-state) and is cluster-gated by GPU memory requirements.
  \item \textbf{DINNDeep does not extrapolate spatially.} Block cross-validation gives held-out Pearson $r{=}0.301$ versus in-distribution $r{=}1.000$ on iron, indicating that DINNDeep is fit-to-training rather than generalizing across spatial blocks. The DINN baseline is the appropriate variant for cross-AOI claims.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

Three priorities.
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Differentiable full Darwin, regional.} Port \texttt{darwin\_plankton.F} to differentiable PyTorch, replacing the box-model proxy. Memory-feasible on existing single-GPU hardware ($\sim$15\,GB for regional steady-state). Tests whether the 5/6 ceiling is structural-to-the-method or structural-to-the-proxy.
  \item \textbf{Time-resolved fitting.} Multi-year dynamical recovery instead of climatological steady-state. Cluster-gated via cluster GPU memory; the relevant compute access is currently being pursued via MIT ORCD's AICR (NVIDIA B200) beta program.
  \item \textbf{Satellite observation channels.} Shelved for this paper to keep the v05 framing canonical; PACE 2024 ocean-color and MODIS particulate inorganic carbon loaders are built and parked for the leapfrog companion paper.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We adapt the BINN architecture \citep{xu2025binn} from terrestrial soil carbon to marine biogeochemistry, building a differentiable per-cell parameter recovery framework that replaces ECCO-Darwin's Green's-functions calibration with gradient-based inference. Across 857 seeds in 86 configurations of multi-AOI joint training, the framework recovers the Basin C iron pair reproducibly (38/40 Cal-grade) and exhibits a structural 5/6 ceiling on full Carroll-N recovery --- supported by four independent pieces of evidence and consistent with a parameter-conservation framing. The ceiling is a diagnostic rather than a method failure: it predicts that observation channels orthogonal to the current set would unblock 6/6 recovery, and DarwinDiff's framework makes such tests cheap. The methodological promise of differentiable parameter recovery for ocean biogeochemistry --- gradient-cheap calibration with swappable observation channels --- is delivered.
