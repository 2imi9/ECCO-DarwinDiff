**Named projects.**
- **MAESSTRO (Masked Autoencoders for Sea Surface Temperature Reconstruction under Occlusion)** — Goh et al., 2024 (DOI 10.5194/os-20-1309-2024). Uses masked autoencoders (MAE) to fill cloud-cover gaps in 1 km resolution sea surface temperature (SST) fields and reconstruct high-resolution SST imagery, including detection of small-scale SST fronts relevant to ocean heat, carbon, and nutrient exchange.  _Data:_ High-resolution (1 km, ~1 km GHRSST-class) sea surface temperature observations plus numerical simulations used for training.  _ML:_ Masked autoencoder (MAE) trained to learn 'the anisotropic nature of small-scale ocean fronts' and reconstruct masked SST imagery.  _BGC:_ Purely physical SST reconstruction. The project description gestures at fronts being 'relevant to ocean heat, carbon, and nutrient exchange' but does no biogeochemical, carbon-cycle, or biological modeling itself -- carbon/nutrients are mentioned only as motivation, not as a variable it touches.
- **NeurOST (Deep Learning Improves Global Satellite Observations of Ocean Eddy Dynamics)** — Martin et al., 2024 (DOI 10.1029/2024GL110059). Deep learning method that synthesizes multi-modal satellite observations (sea surface height + sea surface temperature) to produce global surface ocean current maps with markedly better resolution/accuracy than the standard altimetry product, improving observation of eddy dynamics and cross-scale energy transfer.  _Data:_ Multi-modal satellite sea surface height (altimetry) and sea surface temperature observations.  _ML:_ Deep learning fusion of multi-modal satellite fields; reported 'up to a 30% improvement in spatial resolution over the community-standard sea surface height product.'  _BGC:_ None. Purely physical circulation/eddy-dynamics reconstruction; no biological, chemical, or carbon variables involved.
- **SSBI -- 'Small Scales, Big Impacts' (ArcGIS StoryMap, arcg.is/1aery81)** — Lead author Qiyu Xiao; co-author Spencer Jones. Uses a CNN (U-Net) trained on high-resolution simulations (an idealized channel model, then ECCO) to reconstruct ocean vorticity, divergence, and strain from raw sea surface height, in order to separate SWOT's 'balanced' geostrophic motion from 'unbalanced' internal-gravity-wave signal. Trained on ~35,000 ECCO daily SSH snapshots (Sep-Oct 2011) in the Agulhas Current region, using Lagrangian-filtered flow (Jones et al. 2023) as a target to remove wave signal. Cites Xiao, Balwada, Jones et al. (2023), JAMES, doi:10.1029/2023MS003709 and Jones, Xiao, Abernathey & Smith (2023), JAMES, doi:10.1029/2022MS003220.  _Data:_ SWOT sea surface height (motivating the method); training data from ECCO (MITgcm state estimate) daily SSH snapshots and an idealized channel model simulation, both purely physical ocean-circulation fields.  _ML:_ Convolutional Neural Network, specifically a U-Net (encoder-decoder with skip connections), trained to map SSH -> vorticity/divergence/strain, both raw and Lagrangian-filtered.  _BGC:_ None. Explicitly framed as reconstructing physical kinematics (vorticity, divergence, strain) to recover geostrophic vs. wave motion. No tracer, plankton, carbon, or ocean-color content anywhere in the story.
- **Finding Fronts (ArcGIS StoryMap, arcg.is/0Gyu9S1)** — Lead author Ethan Cruz. Compares three traditional ocean-front edge-detection algorithms (Canny, Cayula-Cornillon, Belkin-O'Reilly) against a machine-learning approach (Gaussian Mixture Model, GMM) for detecting ocean fronts in the Gulf of Mexico across four satellite variable types, in both summer and winter conditions, and again at higher resolution using PACE + SWOT in 2024. Explicitly ties fronts to ecology: 'ocean fronts are important because they attract marine life... often marked by high concentrations of chlorophyll, a green pigment found in marine algae known as phytoplankton,' and shows chlorophyll-a maps tied to river-nutrient-driven phytoplankton blooms and Loop Current eddies.  _Data:_ Four satellite-derived variables: sea surface salinity (SMAP), sea surface height (SWOT and altimetry), sea surface temperature, and chlorophyll-a (PACE ocean color, used explicitly at both Gulf-wide and fine (Yucatan Peninsula) scale in April/August 2024).  _ML:_ Gaussian Mixture Model (unsupervised clustering into water-mass distributions, with an 'I-metric' interface-probability field) compared against three classical gradient/statistics-based edge-detection algorithms (Canny, Cayula-Cornillon, Belkin-O'Reilly).  _BGC:_ The ONLY project on the site that touches ocean-color biology: it uses PACE chlorophyll-a (a phytoplankton proxy) as one of four input fields for ML-based front detection, and discusses phytoplankton blooms/nutrient delivery as ecological motivation. However, the ML task itself is physical (locating water-mass boundaries/fronts), not biogeochemical process modeling -- there is no carbon-cycle variable, no plankton growth/grazing dynamics, no nutrient budget, and chlorophyll is used only as a passive tracer field to be edge-detected, the same as temperature or salinity.

**Datasets.** ECCO 1-Degree Reanalysis (ECCO V4r4, 1992-01-01 to 2018-01-01, 1 deg llc90 grid, >3 TB, NASA PO.DAAC) -- physical ocean/sea-ice state estimate; ECCO-LLC4320 (1/48 deg high-resolution nature run, hourly output, ~5 PB for ~14 months; public Pre-SWOT subset 2011-09-13 to 2012-11-14; NASA NAS ECCO Data Portal / PO.DAAC) -- physical; Ka-Band Interferometry Sea Surface Height / SWOT Level 2 KaRIn Low Rate (120 km swath, 2 km and 250 m grid options, active from 2022-12-16, science orbit since Aug 2023; NASA PO.DAAC) -- physical sea surface height; GHRSST Level-4 MUR Sea Surface Temperature v4.1 (0.01 deg / ~1 km global daily, 2002-06-01 to present; NASA PO.DAAC) -- physical SST; Aquarius Sea Surface Salinity, Official Release Level 3 V5.0 (1 deg gridded, daily-to-annual composites, mission 2011-08-25 to 2015-06-07; NASA PO.DAAC) -- physical salinity; SMAP Sea Surface Salinity, RSS Level 3 V6.0 (0.25 deg grid, ~70 km resolution, 8-day running mean, active from 2015-03-27; NASA PO.DAAC) -- physical salinity; PACE Ocean Color Data (hyperspectral, ~1.2 km resolution, ~2-day global revisit, launched 2024-02-08, public data from 2024-04-11; NASA OB.DAAC) -- explicitly described as 'for phytoplankton, water quality, aerosols, clouds, and biogeochemical applications'; this is the one BGC/ocean-color dataset on the datasets page, though no listed project uses it for anything beyond chlorophyll-a as a front-detection input (Finding Fronts)

**BGC gap verdict (site).** Almost entirely physical oceanography, with exactly one partial, narrow exception. Of the four named projects: MAESSTRO (SST gap-filling via masked autoencoder) and SSBI/"Small Scales, Big Impacts" (CNN/U-Net reconstruction of vorticity/divergence/strain from SWOT-style sea surface height, trained on ECCO SSH) are purely physical -- no biology, chemistry, or carbon variable appears anywhere in either. The ONE exception is "Finding Fronts": it uses PACE ocean-color chlorophyll-a as one of four input fields (alongside SSS, SSH, SST) to compare a Gaussian Mixture Model against classical edge detectors for locating ocean fronts in the Gulf of Mexico, and it explicitly discusses chlorophyll/phytoplankton and nutrient-driven blooms as ecological motivation for why fronts matter. But even there, chlorophyll-a is used only as a passive tracer to be edge-detected -- exactly like temperature or salinity -- not as an input to any biogeochemical process model; there is no carbon-cycle variable (no DIC/ALK/pCO2/POC/PIC), no plankton growth or grazing dynamics, and no parameter-recovery or process-model component anywhere in the story. The datasets page lists one BGC-flavored product, PACE Ocean Color (explicitly captioned 'for phytoplankton, water quality, aerosols, clouds, and biogeochemical applications'), but no dataset for GLODAP, BGC-Argo, ocean carbon, or export production appears anywhere on datasets.htm.
