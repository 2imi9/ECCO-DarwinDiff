# Findings — 2026-05-02

## Identifiability is per-parameter and per-observation-type

The central scientific finding from this round of prototype work. Joint recovery of four parameters of a closed-loop NPZ system (μ uptake rate, m_lin linear mortality, m_quad quadratic mortality, r_remin remineralisation fraction) gives sharply different results depending on what is observed:

| Parameter | Steady-state observations | Time-resolved observations | Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| μ | 79 % rel RMSE | 11 % | 7× |
| m_lin | 46 % | 22 % | 2× |
| m_quad | 47 % | 42 % | 1.1× |
| r_remin | 308 % | 88 % | 3.5× |

Time-resolved observations (five snapshots through the integration rather than a single final snapshot) dramatically improve μ recovery, moderately improve m_lin and r_remin, and barely improve m_quad. The structural reason is that closed-loop cycling lets parameters trade off against each other when only the steady state is observable; the cycling rate itself only constrains the trajectory shape.

r_remin retains a residual partial degeneracy with the mortality terms because it enters the equations multiplicatively (recycle = r_remin × mortality). Even time-resolved data does not fully break this. Pinning down r_remin would require an additional observation type (POC export flux, total mass), not better statistics on N and P.

Implication for real Darwin: BGC-Argo time series will identify rate parameters that ship-cruise snapshots cannot; some parameter pairs will need additional observation types regardless of time resolution. Identifiability has to be assessed per parameter against the available observation set, not assumed.

## Recovery behaviour of the differentiable scaffold

In addition to the identifiability finding above, the prototype results across simpler systems:

- Single-tracer 1D reaction-diffusion: depth-dependent loss rate λ(z) recovered at **2.3 % rel RMSE** from a single noisy final snapshot.
- Coupled nutrient + phytoplankton, fixed mortality, learning only the depth-dependent uptake rate μ(z): **11.5 %** on μ; observed N fit 1.6 %, observed P fit 10.3 %.
- Same coupled system, joint recovery of μ(z) and depth-dependent mortality(z): **μ 6.1 %, mortality 8.6 %**, N fit 1.4 %, P fit 9.3 %. μ recovery improves over the single-parameter case because the model no longer has to force μ to compensate for a wrong fixed mortality.

In all four notebooks, autograd flows correctly through hand-coded numerical integration of the time-stepped physics, the MLP-predicts-parameter composition is stable across thousands of training steps, and gradient descent converges. Mass conservation holds to within 5 % under full-recycling conditions (forward-Euler numerical drift only) — the integrator is correctly closed.

## Verified parameter inventory of ECCO-Darwin

- **Darwin 1** (Carroll 2020 build): **103** independent tunable scalar parameters. Carroll et al. 2020 calibrated **6** of them via the Green's functions method (iron dust solubility, iron scavenging rate, small phytoplankton growth rate, large phytoplankton growth rate, diatom palatability, PIC/POC ratio) — **5.8 % calibration coverage**. The remaining 94 % are held at expert or literature defaults. One of those (the 100-day fixed DOC remineralisation rate) is explicitly flagged by Savelli et al. 2026 as a real limitation of the current calibration.
- **Darwin 3** (recent rework, used in v05 and v06 setups): **100** distinct tunable parameter names exposed via namelist, **657** individual values when arrays are expanded over plankton, group, and matrix dimensions. Darwin 3 is namelist-driven — spatially-varying parameter learning does not require source recompilation, in contrast to Darwin 1 where most kinetic parameters live in hardcoded Fortran.

Full per-parameter listings with file and line references for both Darwin 1 and Darwin 3: `docs/ecco_darwin_parameter_inventory.md`.
